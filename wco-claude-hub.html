Module 01 · Foundation

Getting
started

What Claude is, how it thinks, and how WealthCo has set it up for you. Your first 15 minutes.

What is Claude?

Claude is an AI assistant made by Anthropic. Think of it as a highly capable thinking and writing partner — not a search engine, not autocomplete, not a chatbot that gives robotic answers. It reasons, writes, questions, and adapts to context. The more specifically you direct it, the better it performs.

01
It thinks in long form
Claude doesn't just retrieve facts — it reasons through problems, weighs considerations, and structures arguments. Ask it to think through a complex client situation, not just summarise it.
02
It challenges, not just validates
Claude is built to push back, surface gaps, and argue against its own output when asked. It's not a yes-machine — use that to your advantage.
03
It's designed for nuanced writing
Claude excels at tone, voice, register, and audience adaptation. It can match WealthCo's challenger tone, shift to warm IA collegial voice, or write plain-language client copy — all from a prompt.
How WealthCo has set it up
01
You have a Team plan seat
You're on WealthCo's Claude Team plan. You have access to claude.ai and Claude Desktop (Cowork). Ensure you've downloaded the Desktop app — this enables file collaboration directly on your computer.
02
Use the Strike Team project
All your work should live inside your Claude project. Projects give Claude persistent context — it understands your role, your tasks, and the WealthCo context without you re-explaining it each time.
03
Sign the governance framework
You've received a DocuSign from Dane Solon. This is your acknowledgement of the WealthCo AI usage governance framework — make sure it's completed before using Claude with any work content.
04
No client PII — this is mandatory
Do not enter client names, SINs, account numbers, or any personally identifiable information into Claude. Anonymise and describe the situation instead. This is a temporary restriction while data lake infrastructure is built.
Your first conversation

The best way to learn Claude is to ask it to teach you. Try this as your first prompt:

Starter prompt

"I'm a [your role] at WealthCo, a Canadian integrated financial advisory firm. I'm new to using Claude professionally. Walk me through the most impactful ways someone in my role can use you — and show me an example of each."

Module 02 · Core skill

Prompting
guide

How to write prompts that consistently get sharp, useful output. Four principles, five techniques.

The four principles
01
Be specific about what you want
Vague prompts produce vague outputs. Specify the format, length, audience, purpose, and any constraints. Claude will fill gaps with assumptions — give it as little to assume as possible.
"Write a 150-word proposal intro for a business owner who sold their company for $4M and is meeting us for the first time. Tone: direct, confident, no platitudes. Audience: 58-year-old male, skeptical of financial advisors."
02
Give Claude a role before the task
Before describing what you want, tell Claude who you want it to be. A role activates the right voice, expertise level, and framing. One line at the top changes everything.
"You are a senior financial writer who specialises in writing for high-net-worth Canadian business owners. Write a..."
03
Iterate, don't restart
The best output rarely comes from one prompt. Stay in the conversation and refine. Ask Claude to sharpen a section, shift the tone, cut by half, or argue against its own draft. Each turn builds on the last.
"Good. Now make the opening sentence more direct. Cut the second paragraph. And give me a shorter alternative for the CTA."
04
Ask Claude to think, not just produce
For complex tasks, ask Claude to think through the approach before writing. "What's the best way to structure this?" or "What am I missing?" produces a much sharper starting point than jumping straight to a draft.
"Before you write anything, tell me how you'd approach this and what you'd need to know to do it well."
Advanced techniques
After Claude delivers a draft or analysis, ask: "What are the weaknesses in this? What did you miss or assume?" Claude is remarkably good at stress-testing its own work. Build this into every review step before sharing anything externally.
Before drafting anything, describe the problem. What are you trying to accomplish, who is the audience, what does success look like? Ask Claude to help you think through the approach before writing a single word.
Share a recommendation, proposal, or decision and ask Claude to argue against it. Not to be contrarian — to surface the weakest points before someone else does. "Here's my recommendation. Play devil's advocate — what are the strongest objections someone could raise?"
Show Claude what you want AND what you don't. Paste in a piece of writing you admire and one you don't. Say "write something more like the first, less like the second." Examples do more work than descriptions.
Long iterative revision sessions accumulate context quickly, which drives up usage. If you've finished one task and are starting a new one, start a fresh conversation. This keeps costs lower and responses sharper.
Common prompting mistakes
✓ Do this
Give a role first, then the task
Specify format, length, audience
Iterate in the same conversation
Ask Claude to challenge its output
Share context and constraints upfront
✕ Avoid this
Starting with "Write me a..."
Accepting the first draft as final
Restarting a fresh conversation to revise
Asking vague questions and hoping
Omitting audience or purpose
Prompt starters
"You are a [role]. Help me [task]."
"Before you write, tell me your approach."
"What are the weaknesses in this?"
"Argue against this recommendation."
"Cut this by half without losing meaning."
"Give me 3 alternative versions."
Usage tip

Iterative back-and-forth in long sessions is the #1 driver of high usage. Split into separate conversations when you move to a new task to keep costs manageable.

Module 03 · Practical

Use cases
at WealthCo

Real applications for your day-to-day work — writing, analysis, client prep, and internal communication.

Writing & communications
01
Draft client follow-ups from meeting notes
Paste in your meeting notes (anonymised) and ask Claude to draft a follow-up email. Specify tone, length, and any key actions to include.
"Here are my notes from a client meeting. Draft a follow-up email that is warm, professional, and summarises the three key next steps we agreed on."
02
Draft proposals from briefs
Give Claude the client context, the objective, and the key points to hit. Ask it to structure and draft the proposal sections, then iterate on each one.
"Here's the brief for a client proposal. Draft the executive summary and situation overview sections. Tone: direct, confident, WealthCo brand."
03
Handle long email threads
Paste in a long email thread and ask Claude to summarise it, extract decisions, or draft a reply that cuts through the noise.
"Here's a long email thread. Summarise the key points, what decisions have been made, and what is still unresolved. Then draft a reply that moves things forward."
04
Write two versions for different audiences
Ask Claude to produce the same content at two register levels — one for a client, one for an internal stakeholder. Saves significant time on adapted messaging.
"Write this message in two versions: one for a client who is not financially sophisticated, one for our internal investment committee."
Analysis & thinking
01
Compress long documents into a decision brief
Turn a lengthy report or policy document into a one-page brief with context, key facts, decision required, and recommended next step.
"Read this document and produce a one-page decision brief: background, the 3 most important facts, what decision needs to be made, and a recommended next step."
02
Identify gaps in documents
Ask Claude what questions a document leaves unanswered. This is one of the most underused prompts — it surfaces blind spots before your clients or leadership do.
"Read this document. What questions does it leave unanswered? What would a skeptical reader ask that isn't addressed here?"
03
Turn scattered notes into structured documents
Paste rough notes, voice memo transcripts, or bullet points — ask Claude to organise them into a structured format without losing anything.
"Here are my rough notes from the strategy session. Turn these into a structured summary with sections for context, decisions made, and open questions."
Client preparation
01
Prep for difficult conversations
Describe a tricky client situation (anonymised) and ask Claude to help you think through angles, anticipate objections, and draft your key talking points before the meeting.
"I have a client meeting tomorrow where I need to address concerns about recent portfolio performance. Help me anticipate their objections and draft clear, honest responses."
02
Explain complex topics in plain language
Ask Claude to translate financial or legal concepts into clear, jargon-free client language. Specify that the client is not financially sophisticated.
"Explain the difference between a family trust and a holding company in plain language a 60-year-old business owner with no tax background would understand."

skills

How to use Claude's brand commands to produce on-brand writing and design for WealthCo and Integrated Advisory.

The slash commands

Type /wealthco-brand or /integrated-advisory-brand at the start of any prompt to activate the full brand guidelines. Claude will apply voice, tone, colour, naming, and design rules automatically.

01
/wealthco-brand
Applies WealthCo brand standards: Tone 4 challenger voice, off-white palette, almost black, asset green accent, banned phrases flagged, sentence case enforced, trigger moment anchoring.
"/wealthco-brand — Write a proposal intro for a client exiting with $4M. Under 150 words."
02
/integrated-advisory-brand
Applies IA brand standards: warm cream background, deep teal gradient, peer/collegial voice for CPAs, CPA-as-orchestrator framing, no sales language, "member firm" terminology.
"/integrated-advisory-brand — Write a LinkedIn post for a CPA firm owner considering advisory services."
WealthCo brand rules
✓ Always do this
Write "WealthCo." with a period in design
Use sentence case for all headings
Lead with the client's trigger moment
Use Tone 4 challenger voice as default
Use one sub-brand accent colour max
Add swoops on dark hero sections
✕ Never do this
Write "WealthCo Inc." in client copy
Use "holistic", "robust", "synergistic", "journey"
Write "trusted advisors" — show outcomes
Lead with WealthCo, not the client
Mix multiple sub-brand accent colours
Add drop shadows or gradients to logo

Module 07 · Admin

Usage &
credits

How Claude billing works at WealthCo, what drives usage up, how the cap hierarchy works, and what to do if you hit your limit.

Two systems, one plan

Think of it like a phone plan. Included usage is your monthly data — it's already paid for and resets every week. Extra usage is like going over your data cap — it kicks in when you exceed your included allocation and costs extra per use.

01
Included usage limit
Every seat on the Team plan comes with a weekly included usage allocation. This resets every week and is already covered by your seat cost — there's no extra charge for usage within this limit. It's per user, not shared.
02
Extra usage spend cap
When you exceed your included weekly allocation, Claude draws on extra usage — which costs real dollars and counts against the org's monthly spend cap. This resets on the 1st of each month. If the org cap is exhausted, extra usage stops for everyone until it resets.
03
Hitting your limit doesn't mean you can't use Claude
If you hit the extra usage cap, your included weekly allocation still works. You just can't go beyond it until either your weekly usage resets or the org cap resets on the 1st. You are never fully locked out.
The three-tier cap hierarchy

There are three nested limits — whichever one is hit first wins.

01
Org-wide cap
The total monthly extra usage budget for the entire org. Set by the org owner (David). Once this is hit, no one in the org can use extra usage until it resets on the 1st.
02
Seat-type cap
A cap applied to all Standard or Premium seats as a group. Currently set to unlimited for both seat types at WealthCo — so this tier isn't actively restricting anyone right now.
03
Individual user cap
A per-person monthly extra usage limit. Protects against one person spending the entire org budget. At WealthCo, only Sophie, Bryton, and Yan have spending allowances enabled — all others are set to zero.
Important: individual caps higher than org cap

If an individual cap is set higher than the org cap, the org cap always wins. For example: if your personal limit is CA$50 but the org cap is CA$200, you can only ever spend up to CA$200 org-wide regardless. Individual limits only meaningfully restrict when they're lower than the remaining org budget.

What drives usage up
01
Long conversations with many back-and-forth turns
Each message in a long conversation re-processes the entire conversation history. A 40-turn conversation consumes significantly more than 40 individual short exchanges. This is the #1 driver of high usage at WealthCo.
02
Large file uploads
Uploading large PDFs, spreadsheets, or documents adds significant context that Claude has to process with every response in that conversation.
03
Iterative revisions in a single conversation
Multiple rounds of "change this, now change that" on a single piece of work in one conversation accumulates context fast. Each revision asks Claude to re-read everything before it responds.
04
Tool-heavy operations (memory, skill files, search)
Every time Claude reads a skill file, writes to memory, or runs a web search, it uses additional processing. Sessions with many tool calls (like generating the brand guide or this hub) run heavier than simple writing tasks.
05
Using Opus instead of Sonnet
Opus is a significantly more powerful (and more expensive) model. For most everyday tasks, Sonnet delivers excellent results at a fraction of the cost. Always check your model setting — it should be set to Sonnet by default.
Simple habits that reduce usage
✓ Do this
Split tasks into separate conversations when you move to a new topic
Use Sonnet (not Opus) for everyday writing and analysis
Start a fresh conversation for each new project or document
Paste summaries rather than entire documents when possible
Finish one task fully before pivoting to another in the same chat
✕ Avoid this
Running 20+ revision rounds on one piece in a single conversation
Uploading large files you don't need Claude to fully process
Switching between unrelated tasks in one long conversation
Leaving Opus selected when Sonnet would do the job
Re-uploading the same document across multiple turns
Self-audit prompt

If you've hit your usage limit or want to understand your usage patterns better, paste this prompt into a new Claude conversation. Claude will analyse your history and give you a usage report.

Self-audit prompt — copy and paste into Claude

I'd like you to analyze my Claude conversation history and produce a usage report to help with credit planning. Please use the "Search past chats" and "Recent chats" tools to review my activity, then provide:

1. Activity summary — how many conversations, and when?
2. Task type breakdown — what am I using Claude for most? Group into categories with rough percentages.
3. Usage patterns — when do I use it most? Any spikes or gaps?
4. Conversation depth — are my conversations short (1–3 turns) or extended?
5. Credit tier assessment — am I a light, moderate, or heavy user?
6. Limit analysis — which sessions appear heaviest? Was hitting my limit a one-off or a pattern?
7. Recommendations — do I need a permanent spending allowance, a buffer, or just habit adjustments?

Be as specific as the data allows, and flag anything you couldn't determine.

What members can and can't see
01
Members cannot see their own spend
Usage analytics are only visible to Org Owners and Primary Owners. Regular team members don't have a personal spend dashboard — they only find out they've hit their limit when Claude stops allowing extra usage. There's no advance warning.
02
Admins can export the spend report
Dane (as org admin) can download an "Export Spend Report" from the Analytics section in Organisation Settings and share it with the team directly. If you want visibility on your usage, reach out to Dane.
03
The self-audit prompt is your best tool
Since you can't see a dollar amount, the self-audit prompt above is the most practical way to understand your own usage patterns, identify heavy sessions, and get a recommendation on whether you need a higher allowance.

Claude usage
reporting guide

A three-step process to analyse usage activity, consolidate reports, and export a final branded PDF. Run once per person, share with Dane.

Purpose

This guide gives every Strike Team member a repeatable, three-step process for analysing Claude usage and producing a final consolidated report — covering both Claude Projects and the main chat. Each step produces its own PDF. The final PDF is a single branded document covering your full usage, ready to share with Dane.

How the flow works
A
Prompt A — run inside each Claude Project → PDF per Project
Each Project has its own conversation history. Run Prompt A inside every Project you use. Claude analyses usage and generates a downloadable PDF for that Project. Repeat for each Project.
B
Prompt B — run in the main Claude chat → PDF for main chat
The main Claude.ai chat (outside any Project) holds a separate history. Run Prompt B there. Claude analyses that usage and generates a second downloadable PDF.
C
Prompt C — upload all PDFs → one final consolidated PDF
Open a fresh chat. Upload all PDFs from steps A and B. Run Prompt C. Claude reads the uploaded reports, merges everything, and generates one final branded PDF — the deliverable you share with Dane.

Tips & troubleshooting
Add this line to the end of the prompt: "Use your best judgment based on available history — do not ask for clarification."
Claude uses built-in search and recent chats tools to read history. If it says these are unavailable, try running the prompt again in a fresh chat. Tool availability can occasionally vary by session.
If Claude produces the report but no link, ask: "Please generate this as a downloadable PDF file and provide a link." If it still fails, ask Claude to output it as a self-contained HTML file instead — open in any browser, then use File → Print → Save as PDF.
Make sure all files were fully uploaded before sending the prompt. If a file is large, wait for the upload indicator to complete. You can also ask: "Please confirm how many PDFs you have received before proceeding."
Skip Prompt B. Run Prompt A once, download the PDF, then upload just that one file for Prompt C.
Add a date range to Prompts A and B: "Focus on activity from [start date] to [end date]."
Claude will flag limited data in the PDF output. Partial data still gives useful directional insight — note the limitation when sharing the consolidated report with Dane.

Brand voice, CPA-focused writing, visual identity, and the slash command guide for Integrated Advisory content.

What is Integrated Advisory™?

Integrated Advisory™ is a professional development model and advisory network for CPA firms in Canada. It helps accountants evolve from compliance-only providers into trusted advisors — the orchestrator at the centre of their clients' financial lives.

01
It speaks to CPAs, not end consumers
Every piece of IA content is written for the experienced accounting professional who is ready to think bigger about their practice — never for their clients directly.
02
The CPA as orchestrator
The core belief: CPAs are the most trusted professional in their clients' lives. IA content helps them activate that trust — moving from answering questions to leading conversations, from filing returns to shaping futures.
03
The enemy: the compliance ceiling
Compliance revenue has a ceiling. IA content consistently names this tension — respectfully, not dismissively — and frames advisory as the path beyond it.

Write for CPAs, not clients

03
Article writing
IA articles follow a structured pattern: opening tension → insight or reframe → practical framework → client-outcome anchor → closing invitation. Ask Claude to follow this.
"/integrated-advisory-brand — Write a thought leadership article for CPA firm owners on the value of becoming an orchestrator. Follow the IA article structure."

IA article structure
01
Opening tension or observation — name a real challenge the audience is experiencing. Never start with "we".
02
The insight or reframe — a new way of seeing the issue through the IA lens.
03
Practical framework or steps — something the CPA can actually use. Questions, frameworks, tactics.
04
Client-outcome anchor — connect back to what this means for business-owner clients.
05
Closing invitation — a gentle but clear call to reflection or action. Not a hard sell.
